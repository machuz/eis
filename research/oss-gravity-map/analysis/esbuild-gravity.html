Insight

esbuild reads as a universe of one. The sole author shaped the whole bundler — Design, Breadth and Indispensability all at 100, across 4,202 commits — and they are the only author the surviving structure leans on at all.

Yet the telescope does not return a gravity of 100. It returns 15.0. The shape is perfect; what is missing is the relational evidence. Gravity is not how much one person owns — it is how much the rest of the system rests on them while others keep pressing on the code. Here no one else ever commits inside the sole author's modules, so robust survival stays at zero, the survival gate holds at its floor, and the field collapses inward. Ownership without contest cannot mint gravity, and that is the instrument declining to confuse the two.

So the 77.0% concentration carries a double reading. It is among the most concentrated universes in the map — below swc (92.0%), far above Kubernetes (27.5%) or Rails (9.8%) — but the concentration is not a verdict on power. It is the visible trace of a structure that nothing external has yet leaned on.

For a focused tool this holds, and holds well: esbuild is fast and coherent because one mind kept every structural decision close. But it leaves a quiet question. If a system rests entirely on one author, and no other hand has ever had to carry that code under pressure — would anyone be able to catch it if that author quietly left?

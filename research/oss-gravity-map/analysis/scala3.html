📐 Methodology

Gravity = catGate(Catalysis) × survGate(RobustSurvival) × shape(0.45·Design + 0.25·Breadth + 0.30·Indispensability)

Gravity is a relational signal. The shape says what an engineer built; the two gates ask whether the rest of the system observably leans on it. Neither a surviving shape alone nor others' activity alone can mint it — that is why founding a language, on its own, does not guarantee a high score once others edit around it. It is not a measure of productivity or commit count.

The two gates & the shape:

  • Catalysis (gate) — whether others build on the surviving foundation. The one axis necessarily zero for a solo author, so it witnesses that the structure leans on someone.
  • RobustSurvival (gate) — whether the code lasts under others' pressure: lines surviving in modules where authors other than you keep committing. Dead-corner or self-churned code does not qualify.
  • Design — ownership of architectural files (types, core modules, interfaces).
  • Breadth — how widely the engineer's surviving code spans the project.
  • Indispensability — how much of the surviving codebase rests on this person alone (bus factor).

In scala3, Odersky's shape saturates (Design 100 / Breadth 100), yet his gates are low (Catalysis 8 / RobustSurvival 3.5), so his gravity reads 3.2 (#4). The instrument is not discounting the founding of Scala — it is declining to confuse "who designed it" with "what the system now leans on."

Insight

Rails looks less like a creator's kingdom than like a civilization where many architects share the same ground — and relational gravity reads that flatness honestly.

David Heinemeier Hansson still sits where the structure was first laid: Design 100, Indispensability 100, the founder's signature on the shape of the system. Yet his Gravity reads a quiet 4.0. Under relational gravity, surviving structure alone does not mint influence — the gates ask whether the code still lasts where others actively commit, and whether someone is observably building on it. DHH's modules are revered, but they sit largely undisturbed; the telescope does not confuse that stillness with the system leaning on him today. Might a founder's centrality fade not by being displaced, but simply by no one needing to lean on it anymore?

Around him the field stays remarkably even. zzak (3.5), Jean Boussier (3.2), and Rafael França (3.0) hold gravity within touching distance of the founder, and the design history runs deep across many hands — Jeremy Kemper (Design 90.5), Rafael França (68.4), Aaron Patterson (67.3). Eight contributors clear Design 35, with the Top-10 averaging 44.4. Rails is "framework-driven" in its outward API, yet internally it reads like a project that no single person holds.

Gravity Concentration of 9.4% — top-3 holding under a tenth of the total — is among the lowest of any universe observed, well below the Rust compiler (24.6%) or Kubernetes (26.2%). In a vast, dispersed collaboration, not even the creator's gravity dominates — the system observably rests on no one in particular. That is not a verdict on anyone. It is the instrument declining to read ownership, or seniority, or being first, as gravity.
